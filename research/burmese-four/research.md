# Four Burmese invasion scenes

Prepared 17 October 2026. Scope AHOM-272–275. Images are modern historical reconstructions; project character sheets establish visual continuity, not authenticated portraits. Exact clothes, ceremonial gestures, buildings and battle staging remain interpretive.

## Sources consulted

- [Burmese invasions of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_invasions_of_Assam): Badan Chandra's 1816 appeal, the 1821 recovery campaign, Mahgarh and the frontier defeat. Includes a different Burmese chronology placing the final conquest in July 1822 rather than the June account. This is secondary orientation, not visual eyewitness evidence.
- [Jorhat district history, Government of Assam](https://jorhat.assam.gov.in/about-us/history-jorhat): explicitly associates Chandrakanta's Mahgarh defeat with Mingimaha Bandula in 1822. The page contains chronology problems elsewhere, so it is corroboration rather than a complete campaign authority.
- [Tripura University history teaching material](https://www.tripurauniv.ac.in/site/images/pdf/StudyMaterialsDetail/BA-4TH%28History%29-History%20of%20Tripura%20and%20Assam.pdf): search-indexed account supports the October Assam Chowki and December Guwahati recovery, Sikh/Hindustani troops and March 1822 Mahgarh camp. Related secondary accounts give January 1822 for Guwahati; the catalogue's December convention is retained with uncertainty noted.
- [Chandrakanta / Sudingphaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudingphaa): comparative chronology surfaced in search, including January recovery and the April Mahgarh battle. Exact battlefield numbers, casualty totals and day-of-month are not visualized.
- Earlier project dossier: research/burmese-british-12-completion.md. Costume designs for Burmese commanders and kings are artistic interpretations; later comparative dress material cannot authenticate a specific 1816 or 1822 outfit.

## Scene decisions

| ID | Selected image | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| 272 | Badan Chandra petitions elderly Bodawpaya at the Konbaung court | ‘Court of Ava’ is a conventional title. No exact Inwa palace reconstruction or youthful Bagyidaw substitution. Purnananda absent. |
| 273 | Chandrakanta's troops enter recovered Guwahati | Selects the December 1821 convention, with December/January variation acknowledged. Sikh soldiers are distinct from Assamese supporters; no modern regiment insignia. |
| 274 | Chandrakanta coordinates withdrawal at Mahgarh while Bandula advances at a distance | No personal duel. Flat Upper Assam setting; ramparts, troop arrangement and each commander's exact position are reconstructed. Tilwa absent. |
| 275 | Tilwa consolidates a captured frontier post after Hadira Chowki | No invented coronation or exact proclamation ritual. Chandrakanta's retreat is distant and anonymous; Bagyidaw remains absent. June–July dating retained. |

Bodawpaya and Bagyidaw belong to different stages and must not be exchanged. Mahgarh and Hadira Chowki are separate defeats. The 1822 military collapse must not be conflated with Yandabo in 1826. Calling Tilwa ‘Raja’ follows the selected account; no documented regalia or ceremony is inferred.

## Visual references and review

Inspected six existing character sheets together: Badan Chandra, Bodawpaya, Chandrakanta, Maha Bandula, Mingimaha Tilwa and Bagyidaw. Only people physically depicted are supplied to each generation. The court scene uses Burmese teak/lacquer forms; Assam scenes use earthen fortifications, bamboo/timber, wet plains and river transport. No modern flags, later British uniforms or monumental fantasy architecture.
