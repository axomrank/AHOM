# Moamoria figures: research and visual-reference dossier

Prepared 16 October 2026. Scope: research all seven catalogue entries and create only the first sheet. These are working visual briefs, not authenticated likenesses. Apparent ages, facial features and unrecorded costume details are artistic choices. Moamoria is a religious/political affiliation across communities, not one uniform ethnicity or costume.

## 1. Mayamara Mahanta — Astabhuj Deva

The catalogue's 1769 office-holder is best identified as Astabhuj Deva (Astabhujdeva/Ashtabhujdev). Bhuyan records his succession after Chaturbhujdeva in 1748, distinguishes his son Gagini Deka Mahanta, and records both men's deaths after the 1770 restoration. The catalogue episode is the elder Mahanta's intervention to spare the captured king. He must not be conflated with Aniruddhadeva, the earlier founder, or with Gagini. Sources: S1, S5, S7.

**Visual brief:** elder spiritual leader, composed and firm; white cotton dhoti and modest white upper garment with a restrained red-woven shoulder cloth adapted from local devotional figures. New grey-haired, short-bearded identity; no royal regalia or weapon. Nath's account stresses the Kala-sanghati's limited ritual apparatus, so no obligatory rosary or sacred thread is invented. No forehead symbol is asserted as documented. Exact age and clothes remain reconstruction. Source: S2; local references below.

## 2. Ragha Neog — Raghab/Raghav Moran, rebel Borbarua

Ragha emerged from the Moran leadership, became Borbarua after the 1769 seizure of power, and died during the royalist counter-coup in April 1770. Sources differ over the precise April date; retain that uncertainty rather than silently replacing the catalogue's 11 April with 14 April. His assumption of office should be distinguished from Ramakanta's kingship. Sources: S1, S4.

**Local identity lead:** the seated man on the RIGHT in the Kuranganayani counter-coup image is explicitly the attacked Ragha. This is stronger identity evidence within the art library than guesses about the two men in the outbreak scene. Practical rebel origins and later office-holding permit separate campaign and court costume studies; don't confuse him with crowned Lakshmi.

## 3. Naharkhora Saikia — Nahar Khora

A Moran leader in the first revolt and father of Ramakanta, associated with Ragha in the challenge to the Ahom government. His death belongs to the 1770 royalist recovery. Radha and Rukmini are also named in the first-phase narrative; their activities should not be erased by reducing the revolt to a single male leader. Source: S4.

**Visual brief:** older field leader, distinct from Ragha and visibly older than the proposed Ramakanta design. Working cloth, headcloth and practical equipment may follow local rebel textiles. The outbreak scene links him at scene level but does not label which foreground man is Naharkhora; panel assignment remains unresolved.

## 4. Ramakanta — Ramananda variant

Naharkhora's son was installed as the rebel king in late 1769 and lost power in 1770. Historical retellings vary between Ramakanta and Ramananda; keep the catalogue's canonical spelling and record the variant rather than create a duplicate person. Sources: S1, S4.

**Visual brief:** distinguish the new ruler from both his father and Ragha. A restrained accession/court version can differ from a campaign version. In the captivity scene the richly crowned foreground prisoner is Lakshmi Singha; he is NOT Ramakanta. The raised sword-bearer on the steps is not individually identified in the metadata. Do not copy either as a verified identity.

## 5. Harihar Tanti — Hawha Tanti

A leader of the later uprising. Baruah identifies Hawha Tanti, also known as Harihar, with control over Majuli and adjoining north-bank territory. This geographic role is more useful for a visual brief than treating all rebel chiefs as interchangeable kings. Source: S3. The catalogue's specific 1786 organisation claim needs further chronicle-level checking.

**Visual brief:** practical organiser in plain handwoven cloth, comparatively modest equipment, individual middle-aged face. The surname alone does not establish a documented outfit or a personal weaving tool. No individual local portrait or named scene was found.

## 6. Bharat Singha — Bharath/Bharat Raja

A later rebel ruler associated with Rangpur, distinct from Ramakanta's first-phase kingship. Baruah describes coinage in his name in the 1790s and contrasts his centre of power with Sarbananda's Bengmara and Harihar's north-bank domain. Source: S3.

**Visual brief:** later rebel ruler with restrained court dress, separately designed identity and age. No individually identified local picture was found; the burning-of-Rangpur scenes are atmosphere/textile references only. Catalogue accession and death-year variants remain pending chronological verification; coin dates are not automatically reign boundaries.

## 7. Sarbananda Singha — Matak ruler at Bengmara

The Tinsukia official history associates him with Rangagarh and the transfer to Bengmara in 1791. The Na-Pukhuri account calls his earlier name Mezera and connects his reign with extensive tank-building. These accounts support a ruler/builder brief rather than an anonymous rebel-soldier design. S3 also records separate Bengmara authority and coinage. Sources: S3, S6.

**Visual brief:** established regional ruler, distinct mature face, practical regional court cloth and modest rank markers. No dedicated local portrait found. Do not invent a precise crown from Ahom royal sheets. The official pages contain compressed and inconsistent dynastic wording; detailed ancestry, treaty titles and exact reign chronology require further checking.
