# Six Moamoria scenes: context and references

Prepared 17 October 2026. Scope: AHOM-266–271, the six remaining unillustrated Moamoria entries. Character sheets provide project continuity, not authenticated portraits. Event staging, clothing and site plans are reconstructions.

## Sources read

- [Tripura University history teaching material](https://tripurauniv.ac.in/site/images/pdf/StudyMaterialsDetail/BA-4TH%28History%29-History%20of%20Tripura%20and%20Assam.pdf), PDF pp. 76 and 87 (zero-based 75 and 86): renewed rebellion, Harihar's 1786 organisation, regional administrations, Bharat, Purnananda at Jorhat and the eventual Matak settlement. The text contains chronological inconsistencies, including 1782/1783 and Gaurinath's death. Do not treat every date as settled.
- [Pallavi Baruah, “A Declining Monarchy in Crisis: The Reign of Guari Nath Singha,” FCH Annals 18](https://www.floridaconferenceofhistorians.org/uploads/1/8/2/8/18284773/fch_vol_18.pdf), PDF pp. 48–49, printed pp. 42–43: later rebellion, Bharat's Rangpur authority and coinage, Sarbananda's Bengmara centre, Harihar/Hawha regional role. This article equates Hawha and Harihar; the university material distinguishes their domains. Preserve the disagreement.
- [Tinsukia district history, Government of Assam](https://tinsukia.assam.gov.in/about-us/history): transfer to Bengmara in 1791 and civic works under Sarbananda. Useful for settlement context, not an exact town plan or royal likeness.
- [Jorhat district history, Government of Assam](https://jorhat.assam.gov.in/about-us/history-jorhat): Purnananda at Dichoi and the process of relocation. Its timeline contains an internal inconsistency involving Gaurinath's death and the later move; use it cautiously.
- [Moamoria rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moamoria_rebellion): secondary orientation for the 1782 convoy attack and the conventionally dated 1805 settlement. Uneven sourcing; exact ambush choreography and diplomatic ceremony are not established by this summary.
- [Matak rajya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matak_rajya): autonomy and tribute framework. Infobox succession dates and narrative chronology are not fully consistent; no exact succession ceremony is asserted.

## Scene decisions

|---|---|---|
| 266 | Gaurinath escapes a torchlit convoy ambush | Distinct from the 1788 downstream flight; route and litter are illustrative. |

| 269 | Sarbananda supervises Bengmara settlement work | Physical town layout and tank setting illustrative; no exact Godha portrait claimed. |
| 270 | Purnananda supervises transfer of records to Jorhat | Administrative transition rather than a claimed single royal arrival; Gaurinath absent because of chronology uncertainty. |
| 271 | Sarbananda receives an envoy about autonomy | Purnananda does not personally attend. Envoy, documents and tribute objects illustrate the agreement, not a documented ceremony or tribute quantity. |

## Continuity and visual review

Inspected existing sheets for Gaurinath, Harihar, Bharat, Sarbananda and Purnananda together before generation. Each supplied sheet represents one person, appearing once per image. Bharat's mustard coat/red sash, Sarbananda's white cloth/green sash, Harihar's plain white/blue sash and Purnananda's elder identity must remain distinct. Gaurinath is not substituted for a rebel king.

Humid Assam landscape; bamboo, reeds, broadleaf woodland, alluvium and river transport. No uniform invented Moamoria ethnicity, modern flags, European castles, modern treaties or fantasy crowns. Non-graphic violence. Specific temple and palace floor plans are not invented as documented evidence.

Exact prompts and supplied reference paths are retained per scene, with generation provenance. Method: built-in image_gen. Review precedes catalogue import.


## AHOM-266 Bihu-troupe revision

User supplied bihu dal.jpg as the visual reference for a Bihu group. The remake uses cream/red woven clothing, women’s dance gestures, male dhol musicians and a pepa to make the festival setting readable. This contemporary cultural photograph guides the visual reconstruction; it does not establish exact 1782 costumes, troupe composition or eyewitness details. Dancers and musicians are portrayed as festival participants caught in the confusion, not identified as rebels merely because they perform Bihu. Original painting retained; final variant and provenance recorded separately after review.
