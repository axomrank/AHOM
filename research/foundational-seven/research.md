# Seven foundational scenes — research and visual decisions

Prepared 17 October 2026. Built-in image_gen; existing project character sheets supplied as image references. These sheets establish project continuity, not authenticated historical faces or verified costumes. Landscapes, building details, staging, unnamed people and military dispositions are interpretive.

## Context and source trail

The master catalogue provides the seven event rows and linked people. Read alongside the local design briefs for Tai Sulai, Tasim Pou, Nangchukham, Nandi, Satyanarayana and Ghiyasuddin Azam Shah. Their simplified official clothing and distinct facial designs take precedence over generic royal or military costumes. Inspected Sudangphaa, Tyao Khamti and Sukhrangphaa sheets, plus existing foundational court and interregnum pictures for project continuity.

Source pages checked:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudangphaa — bibliography points to Gait (1926), pp.82–84, and Barua's Ahom Buranji. Supports elephant-mounted king, slight spear injury, pursuit and commanders' treaty, elephant-catching feast and Kamata expedition. Competing Tai Sulai origin stories should not be made visually definitive.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyao_Khamti — expedition and royal-family chronology. Dates vary; avoid depicting a precisely dated battle or invented duel.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutuphaa — notes modern historians' 1364–1369 interregnum chronology, distinct from the later 1376–1380 gap.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate%E2%80%93Kamata_Kingdom_War — contextual account of western conflict; chronology and Ahom participation need caution.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom — catalogue source for the Chutia episode and inscription-attested rulers.
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/A_History_of_Assam_by_E._A._Gait.pdf — further reading for the chronicle narrative; not a source of authenticated visual likenesses.

## Biome and architecture

Assam floodplain vocabulary: wet alluvial earth, braided rivers, silt/gravel bars, reeds, bamboo/cane, humid broadleaf forest, wet rice fields. Patkai setting: freshwater lake and forested misty foothills. Karatoya setting is a western frontier/Bengal floodplain rather than upper-Assam mountain scenery. No claim of botanical species identification or an exact reconstructed site.

Early building vocabulary: timber posts, bamboo framing, woven split-bamboo walls, thatched roofs, modest raised floors, earthworks and wooden palisades. Exclude later Rang Ghar, Talatal Ghar and brick Kareng Ghar; no Mughal or fantasy palace. Clothing retained from supplied sheets is artistic continuity, with its historical limitations acknowledged.
