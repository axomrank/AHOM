# MUHAMMAD AZAM SHAH — reference and design

Prepared 16 October 2026. Modern artistic reconstruction. See ../mughal-bengal-14-research.md for source access, provenance and limitations. Proposed faces, ages, garment cuts and colours are artistic choices except where explicitly connected to a source portrait description. No individual local scene is treated as an authenticated portrait.

Prince Muhammad Azam Shah, Aurangzeb's son, in Bengal context 1679 around age 26, not his brother Muazzam/Bahadur Shah, not emperor's later 1707 appearance. Young slender man, light warm olive-brown skin, narrow long face resembling father but youthful, prominent fine aquiline nose, dark almond eyes, distinctive prominent ears, neat black moustache and very short fine black beard. Seventeenth-century princely court dress informed by c1675–80 portrait catalogue: gold-embroidered white Mughal jama, pale gold patka, white tight trousers, white turban with restrained pearl ruby emerald ornament, modest pearl necklace and bracelets, gold-toned leather riding shoes, jade-hilt dagger sheathed in waist sash and sheathed court sword. No huge crown or armor. Serious poised expression; specific three-dimensional likeness interpretive.
