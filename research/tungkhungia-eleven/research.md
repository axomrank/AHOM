# Tungkhungia restoration: eleven scenes

Prepared 17 October 2026. Catalogue rows AHOM-255–265. All images are modern historical reconstructions. Project character sheets supply continuity, not authenticated likenesses. Faces, clothes, ceremonial staging, documents and building work are interpretive.

## Sources consulted

- [Itakhuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Itakhuli): campaign and naval operations. The August naval action and subsequent fort capture should not be conflated into a precise single day.
- [Ahom affairs, digitized historical account](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/5258.pdf): recovery of Guwahati in 1682; contextual corroboration surfaced by search.
- [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom): Gadadhar's conflict with the satras. Motives remain debated; avoid claiming one proven personal grievance.
- [Rudra Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudra_Singha): 1713 assembly, proposed Bengal campaign, death in 1714. Planning and camp are depicted; no invasion victory is implied.
- [Siva Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutanpha): Krishnaram's patronage, royal religion and Sarbeswari's authority. Her earlier name varies across accounts.
- [Hastividyarnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastividyarnava): elephant treatise and attribution to Sukumar Barkaith and painters Dilbar and Dosai. Workshop staging and their faces are invented conservatively.
- [Namdang, Government of Assam](https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/tourist-place-detail/334): 1703 commission and Bengal workmen. The bridge is depicted as a low stone crossing. Rangnath Dol is a separate site and is not placed beside it.
- [Kareng Ghar, Government of Assam](https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/tourist-place-detail/328): Rajeswar's rebuilding dated 1752. Other accounts give 1758; painting uses a general 1750s worksite and does not invent seven visible aboveground floors.
- [Kirti Chandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirti_Chandra_Borbarua) and [Buranji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buranji): court influence and selective chronicle destruction. Exact timing and dramatic details remain uncertain.

## Scene choices

255: anonymous troops at Itakhuli; Gadadhar is not placed personally on the battlefield.

256: confiscation and displacement at a modest satra, non-graphic; king absent. This distinguishes coercion from the existing peaceful royal visits.

257: Namdang construction alone, avoiding a composite of geographically separate monuments.

258: Rudra with ministers and correspondence, preparing rather than invading Bengal.

259: Rudra ill at Guwahati with Siva; sleeping cloth replaces ceremonial costume. Exact words are not visualized.

260: Krishnaram and Siva in a conservative Nilachal temple courtyard; grant presentation illustrates patronage rather than an authenticated ceremony.

261: painters and writer at separate folios; Siva and Ambika observe. Elephant miniatures are decorative reconstructions, not facsimiles or readable historical text.

262: Sarbeswari receiving state papers as Bar Raja; no other queens shown.

263: Rajeswar seated with Kirti Chandra near the dais; deceased Pramatta absent. No invented European coronation ritual.

264: brick and lime work with bamboo scaffolding at Garhgaon; palace massing interpretive.

265: selected loose bark folios destroyed while others survive. No claim all chronicles were burned or that the king personally attended.

## Visual constraints

Humid Assam landscape, natural anatomy, period materials, distinct extras, one appearance per referenced character. No modern flags, modern bound books, invented readable historical writing, European castles or desert scenery. Use masonry only where appropriate to the period and site.

Exact generation prompts are saved separately per scene. Built-in image_gen is the generation method.
