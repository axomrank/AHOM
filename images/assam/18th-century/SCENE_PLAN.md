# Scene plan — 18th-century Ahom Assam

Created: 26 October 2026  
Status: proposed scenes; research and visual reference selection precede generation.

## 1. Period and purpose

Build a coherent collection of inhabited places: recognisable monuments in use, royal administration, workers, households, streets and rural surroundings. Continue the existing collection's attention to everyday life, rather than making only empty monument portraits.

The user's requested anchor is the building period of Rang Ghar and Kareng Ghar. Those surviving masonry forms belong to the **18th century**, so this folder is named accordingly. The 17th century is 1601–1700; the 18th is 1701–1800.

| Subject | Historical basis | Decision for this collection |
|---|---|---|
| Rang Ghar, Rangpur/Joysagar | Official sources give 1744 or 1746 for the brick pavilion under Pramatta Singha. | Describe its construction as **1740s** until an exact-year brief is resolved; completed-building scenes can use circa 1760. |
| Kareng Ghar, Garhgaon | ASI dates the earlier timber palace to 1540 and the surviving multistorey masonry building to **1752**, under Rajeswar Singha. | Use the surviving building as an architectural anchor for scenes after 1752; do not backdate it to the 1600s. |
| Talatal Ghar, Rangpur | A separate palace/citadel site, with earlier perishable construction and later brick work under Rajeswar Singha. | Research the particular phase before showing a complete reconstruction; keep it distinct from Garhgaon Kareng Ghar. |
| Ordinary homes and settlements | Monument construction does not establish the form of every dwelling. | Research household scale, materials and costume separately; a masonry palace does not turn the whole town into brick architecture. |

**Working setting for the core series:** circa 1755–1765, with circa 1760 as a convenient creative date. This is a production choice, not evidence that every proposed activity or architectural detail is documented for that year.

- `01_ahom_royal_and_civic/kareng-ghar/`: Garhgaon palace references.
- `01_ahom_royal_and_civic/talatal-ghar/`: Rangpur palace/citadel references.
- `05_water_and_landscape/`: water and vegetation only, after screening modern context.
- `03_hindu_temple_architecture/`: only for a specifically named Hindu temple scene.

These are search starting points, not a claim that every image has been authenticated. Before generation, inspect each chosen photograph and its adjacent `.source.json`; record its reference ID, source, attribution and narrow visual role. Exclude unresolved images in `90_needs_identity_review/` from building-identity decisions.

Reuse the fixed-elements library selectively for object form, ecology and visual consistency. Check period suitability first. Previous generated scenes can guide style and continuity, but cannot supply historical evidence.

Keep consistent faces, house plans and costumes within a connected scene set. Existing named character designs are creative assets: check the historical person's lifetime and role before casting them in circa 1760. Do not carry the 15th-century Bhuyan men's clothing rule into this separate Ahom collection. Existing navy guard uniforms likewise require a fresh period decision rather than an automatic authenticity claim.

## 5. Visual direction and constraints

- Realistic materials and inhabited spaces, with natural Assam light, seasonal vegetation and believable human scale. Use a consistent landscape format for connected wide shots; final aspect ratio can be chosen at generation time.
- Show monuments as functioning buildings, while documenting every conjectural restoration of finishes, roofs, furnishings or missing fabric. Present-day exposed brick and ruin damage do not automatically describe their original appearance.
- Keep Rangpur and Garhgaon geographically separate. Do not put Rang Ghar, Garhgaon Kareng Ghar and Talatal Ghar side by side in an invented composite skyline.
- Keep Hindu temple towers, sanctums and deity sculpture within the temple-specific brief. Royal patronage does not make them a palace or common-house template.
- Exclude modern tourist lawns and paths unless historical evidence supports them, railings, signboards, electric infrastructure, vehicles, plastic, modern concrete and contemporary visitor facilities.
- Preserve mixed-influence and memorial labels for Ghanashyam's House and Fakuwa Doul; neither is a generic Ahom home or palace template.
- State uncertainty explicitly for street plans, crowds, colours, everyday dress, ceremony and other reconstructed details.

## 6. Production and record keeping

1. Choose the scene's named site, approximate year and building phase.
2. Assemble a brief separating **source-backed features**, **comparative references** and **creative interpretation**.
3. Inspect selected local reference photographs and metadata; lock architecture before adding elaborate activity.
4. Generate the architectural establishing view first, then use it for continuity in the related human-scale scenes.
5. Review identity, proportions, date, clothing, object scale and modern intrusions; save revisions with their prompts.
6. Store images in the scene group, with `generation-notes.md` recording sources, reference roles, uncertainty, prompts and versions. Add a collection manifest once image files exist, including paths, dates, status, provenance and checksums.

## 7. If we also make a true 17th-century collection

Create a separate `../17th-century/` collection when developing the 1600s. Candidate briefs are a researched timber palace/administrative compound at Garhgaon, ordinary households, river transport, and a specifically dated military or civic scene. These are research proposals, not settled reconstructions. The surviving 1740s Rang Ghar and 1752 Garhgaon palace must not appear in those scenes. Any proposed earlier Rang Ghar needs its own dating and form evidence.

## 8. Checked sources and limits

Sources checked on 26 October 2026:

- [ASI Guwahati Circle — Ahom Raja's Palace](https://www.asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/ahom_raja_palace.html): distinguishes the 1540 timber palace from the surviving 1752 building; describes the four-storey surviving structure. Use ASI's architectural description rather than silently adopting conflicting storey counts elsewhere.
- [Sivasagar district — Rang Ghar](https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/index.php/tourist-place-detail/276): gives 1744, describes the two-storey pavilion and recreational use.
- [Ministry of Tourism, Incredible India — Rang Ghar](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/assam/sivasagar/rang-ghar): gives 1746. The disagreement warrants a 1740s label for now.
- [India's UNESCO Tentative List submission — Rangpur-Sivasagar Historic Ensemble](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7033/): supports the distinction between perishable and later brick palace phases and describes the ensemble's functions. It is a State Party submission on the Tentative List, not a World Heritage inscription or independent verification of every claim. Cross-check detailed dates and reconstructed plans before use.

This plan establishes the monument chronology and a creative production sequence. Household, costume, craft and construction details still need scene-specific research.
