## Reuse and historical limits

Use `web_file` for the website and retain the associated caption and uncertainty notes. `file` points to the native PNG original, except the two unchanged real photographs. Native generated sizes are 1672 × 941, with one 1672 × 940; these are not 4K files. Most everyday scenes are circa 1750, military scenes circa 1670, Joysagar uses Rudra Singha’s reign, and monument comparisons circa 1760. This multi-period collection is kept outside a single-century folder to avoid mislabelling.

Nineteen local references are copied byte-for-byte into `references/`, each with a `.source.json`. The original real-photo catalogue, image bytes, attribution and move ledger are untouched. Generated concepts and object assets are clearly identified as artistic continuity references, never historical evidence. Some comparative-photo licences are not established; those copies are for local research. The two modern monument photographs carry their Commons licences and attribution in the gallery and metadata; they are not claimed to have been taken in 2026.

All scenes are research-informed interpretations, not authenticated restorations. Many exact costumes, workshop mechanisms, household plans, market transactions, crowd compositions and construction details remain speculative. Read the per-image notes. No surviving eighteenth-century palace is projected into a fifteenth-century scene, and Hindu temple, secular palace and funerary architecture remain separate. Seven correction passes are recorded; older attempts remain under the original generation bundle’s `superseded/` folder and the generator’s original save paths.

The main website has not been published or automatically populated with these assets. This collection is reviewable locally and supplies images, captions, alt descriptions, source bindings and optimized WebP copies for later integration.
