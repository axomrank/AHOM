# Research and interpretation notes

Checked 26 October 2026.

## Evidence policy

The collection depicts people living in the Ahom kingdom, not a claim that every object, occupation or person is exclusively Tai-Ahom. Most scenes use circa 1750; military scenes use circa 1670 because the naval documentation concerns that period. Joysagar uses Rudra Singha’s reign because official accounts conflict on its year. Palace comparison uses circa 1760, after ASI’s 1752 date for the surviving Garhgaon masonry.

Local real photographs supply limited material, object and building comparisons. Modern comparative photographs do not independently authenticate early modern practice. Previous generated objects and household scenes supply creative continuity only. Clothing, gestures, domestic plans, workshop layouts, cargo and market transactions remain interpretive. No Bhuyan or Kachari costume direction is generalized to this collection. No Hindu temple is used as an Ahom secular house template. No later palace enters an earlier scene.

## Sources

### S1: Old Handicrafts industries in Assam

[Old Handicrafts industries in Assam](https://industriescom.assam.gov.in/portlet-innerpage/old-handicrafts-industries-in-assam)

Government of Assam craft survey. Supports weaving, pottery, smithing, bamboo work and carpentry as regional traditions; explicitly discusses eighteenth-century silk trade. Not a dated plan for a workshop or costume.

### S2: Miscellaneous Arts & Crafts of Assam

[Miscellaneous Arts & Crafts of Assam](https://ignca.gov.in/divisionss/janapada-sampada/northeastern-regional-centre/miscellaneous-arts-crafts-of-assam/)

Regional cultural survey identifies carpenters making houses, ploughs, looms and boats. Contemporary observations are comparative evidence for materials and craft vocabulary, not proof of an unchanged eighteenth-century technique.

### S3: Textiles of Assam

[Textiles of Assam](https://ignca.gov.in/divisionss/janapada-sampada/northeastern-regional-centre/textiles-of-assam/)

Regional textile overview distinguishes plains posted looms from backstrap looms. Use a timber frame loom; neither the exact pattern nor the assembled workshop is authenticated.

### S4: Jakhoi and Khaloi: Introduction

[Jakhoi and Khaloi: Introduction](https://www.dsource.in/resource/jakhoi-and-khaloi/introduction)

Design documentation of bamboo craft in Barpeta and Nalbari. Comparative reference for basket weave and fishing gear. Does not date specific forms to the Ahom period.

### S5: Traditional Craft and Gears in Assam and Different Fishing Methods

[Traditional Craft and Gears in Assam and Different Fishing Methods](https://rkbassam.aau.ac.in/traditional_craft_and_gears_in_assam_and_different_fishing_methods)

Assam Agricultural University documentation of traditional small wooden boats and manual propulsion. Modern documentation only; exclude engines, plastic and modern boat covers.

### S6: Lachit Barphukan and His Times, S. K. Bhuyan

[Lachit Barphukan and His Times, S. K. Bhuyan](https://lgbgirlscollege.ac.in/online/attendence/lib/1679733063.pdf)

Historical study, printed p.150: describes seventeenth-century dockyards and distinguishes boat-building and boat-plying khels. Supports military preparation themes, not an exact vessel reconstruction or a uniform regulation.

### S7: A History of Assam, Edward Gait

[A History of Assam, Edward Gait](https://ocrdigitalfile.nvli.in/cslrepository/4050/RB847-ocr.pdf)

Indexed historical text includes a translated seventeenth-century description of Assamese war boats and abundant water transport. Use only as corroboration of river traffic; do not turn reported boat counts into established measurements.

### S8: Publication, Directorate of Historical and Antiquarian Studies

[Publication, Directorate of Historical and Antiquarian Studies](https://dhas.assam.gov.in/portlets/publication-0)

Official catalogue identifies published Ahom, Assamese, Kachari and Jayantia chronicles and the Tungkhungia Buranji translation. Establishes the documentary tradition; does not reconstruct a particular writing room.

### S9: Manuscript Painting of Assam: Historical and Contemporary Perspectives

[Manuscript Painting of Assam: Historical and Contemporary Perspectives](https://www.sahapedia.org/manuscript-painting-of-assam-historical-and-contemporary-perspectives)

Scholarly overview identifies sanchi bark and cotton writing supports, preparation through curing/seasoning/polishing and traditional ink. Use oblong unbound bark folios. No invented legible historical passage.

### S10: In Conversation with Chittaranjan Borah

[In Conversation with Chittaranjan Borah](https://www.sahapedia.org/manuscript-painting-of-assam-conversation-chittaranjan-borah)

Practitioner interview on sanchi-bark manuscript practice. Contemporary technical comparison; does not establish the dress or room layout of an eighteenth-century scribe.

### S11: Ahom Raja’s Palace, ASI

[Ahom Raja’s Palace, ASI](https://www.asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/ahom_raja_palace.html)

ASI distinguishes the 1540 impermanent palace from the surviving masonry edifice dated 1752. The historical comparison uses circa 1760. Maintained finishes and lost surroundings are interpretive.

### S12: Rang Ghar, Incredible India

[Rang Ghar, Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/assam/sivasagar/rang-ghar)

Ministry of Tourism identifies Pramatta Singha, the two-storey sports pavilion and its boat-like roof. Circa 1760 is later than his reign. Modern park paths must not enter the historical view.

### S13: The Architecture of Ahoms, INTACH documentation panels

[The Architecture of Ahoms, INTACH documentation panels](https://heritageawards.intach.org/uploads/pdf/Panels_TheArchitectureofAhoms%2CSivasagar%2CAssam_1735205070.pdf)

Architectural documentation, indexed Rang Ghar panel, describes the secular pavilion and arches. Use to corroborate building identity and form, not to convert temple forms into houses.

### S14: Joysagar Tank and Temples, Sivasagar district

[Joysagar Tank and Temples, Sivasagar district](https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/tourist-place-detail/329)

District account attributes the tank to Rudra Singha in memory of Joymoti but gives 1707; other official accounts give 1697. Use the reign, circa 1696–1714, rather than resolve the discrepancy by assertion. Labour arrangement, excavation tools and staging are interpretive.

### S15: Sivasagar District Disaster Management Plan 2025–26

[Sivasagar District Disaster Management Plan 2025–26](https://asdma.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/swf_utility_folder/departments/asdma_revenue_uneecopscloud_com_oid_70/menu/document/sivasagar_2025_26.pdf)

Historical tourism section gives Joysagar as 1697 and Rang Ghar under Pramatta Singha. Other palace details conflict with ASI, so this document is not used to date the palace.

### S16: Political Ecology of an Environmental Crisis in the Brahmaputra Valley, Assam

[Political Ecology of an Environmental Crisis in the Brahmaputra Valley, Assam](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/343185/files/The%20Political%20Ecology.pdf)

Scholarly discussion of historical flood control traces Ahom embankment building and later expansion. Supports an earthwork maintenance theme, not the location, dimensions or performance of the depicted bund.

## Access limitations

Some official pages/PDFs were available through indexed search text but their full endpoints timed out or refused access. ASI palace, IGNCA craft/textile, INTACH panels, Bhuyan naval study and Gait are used only for the specific indexed passages described above. The obsolete Gauhati University PDF path returned 404; a moved-host attempt failed, and that document is NOT counted as consulted evidence. No unsupported date was filled from it. The library’s original bytes, catalogue, attribution and move ledger are untouched.

## Modern photograph comparison

The two modern views are byte-identical copies of local Commons photographs. They are labelled modern reference photographs, not live 2026 photographs. Licence and credit are supplied in the gallery. Generated historical views match the photograph approximately, not as a measured restoration.

### S17: A Study of Markets, Trade Routes, and Currency under the Ahom Government — Raja Doley (2025)

[A Study of Markets, Trade Routes, and Currency under the Ahom Government — Raja Doley (2025)](https://www.ijssr.com/papers/volume-2/issue-4/ijssr30443/)

Historical article discusses hats, goods and river/overland trading routes. Supports the market theme; does not supply stall plans, exact dress, a rice-for-cloth exchange rate or an exclusively barter economy.

### S18: Commerce, Merchants and Markets in the Brahmaputra Valley c.1800–1900 — Nabanita Sharma (2019)

[Commerce, Merchants and Markets in the Brahmaputra Valley c.1800–1900 — Nabanita Sharma (2019)](https://www.granthaalayahpublication.org/journals/index.php/granthaalayah/article/view/17_IJRG19_A11_2889)

Study of pre-colonial/colonial transition based on chronicles and archival sources. Used as a caution against flattening changing commerce into a timeless village-market stereotype. Its nineteenth-century evidence does not directly date a scene to 1750.

## Visual review and remaining limits

Thirty final generated scenes were inspected. Seven edits corrected obvious reference leakage or invented content: aquatic plant mats, generic horned anvil, modern shirts on two background boats, an already-filled lake behind the Joysagar excavation, an invented ball game and a metal grille at Rang Ghar. Prior attempts are excluded from the gallery and retained in the generation bundle. Small tools, cloth details, boat fastening, plant species, modern museum analogies and reconstructed architecture cannot be fully authenticated from these references. The boatbuilding scene retains a small blue-toned binding/detail on its background boat. The cannon, timber-frame and thatching scenes are not technical demonstrations. Crowd and workforce composition is speculative, especially at Joysagar; no demographic or paik-organisation claim is made. Monument geometry and finishes are approximate, with some retained weathering. These limits are recorded beside each affected scene.
