# Assamese jewellery additions

Created 25 October 2026, using built-in imagegen. Eight separate transparent PNG assets with realistic 3D appearance. They are raster illustrations rather than mesh models, manufacturing drawings or certified historical reconstructions.

## Research basis

[Assam State Rural Livelihoods Mission](https://asrlms.assam.gov.in/frontimpotentdata/asomi-product-assamese-jewellery) identifies golpata, lokaparo, dugdugi, keru and thuriya among Assamese ornament types. [Craft Revival Trust / India InCH](https://www.indiainch.org/craft/jewellery-of-assam/) describes gamkharu as a broad clasped bangle and lokaparo as paired birds, and distinguishes several other ornament families. A [SAARC Culture research volume](https://saarcculture.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/vol6_2018.pdf) provides additional jewellery terminology. These sources establish categories, not the date or exact design of the generated examples.

Photographed pieces were inspected together before prompting. Seven named examples come from [Zangfai's Assamese artisan catalogue](https://zangfai.in/); keru comes from [AssamVilla](https://assamvilla.com/product/assamese-traditional-keru-pendant-jewellery-set/). Full per-piece product URLs and selected image URLs are preserved in selected-references.json and keru-reference.json. These are contemporary commercial examples. Source photographs remain local research material and are excluded from the delivery ZIP. Generated compositions omit their display busts, people, logos, lettering and backgrounds.

## What each file represents

- Golpota: a short floral gold-and-red collar, following the catalogue's Projapoti Golpota example. Only the collar is generated, with red fastening ties.
- Gamkharu: two very broad engraved silver cuff bangles, following the photographed silver variant. The visible opening and clasp construction are interpreted from the photograph.
- Muthikharu: the narrower silver Ujjoni Axom example, with elongated leaf relief and floral panels. This preserves its regional catalogue identification rather than treating all muthi kharu as one design.
- Lokaparo: a red-green necklace variant with paired bird motifs and smaller related medallions; the separate photographed earrings are omitted.
- Dugdugi: a heart/pointed-leaf pendant on a red-green bead chain. The distinguishing shape is kept separate from jonbiri's crescent and dholbiri's drum form already in the library.
- Jethipoti: specifically the contemporary blue-green silver Jethipota variant named by the artisan, with a radiating leaf pendant. It is not presented as the sole historical jethipoti form; research descriptions also document medallion bands and other constructions.
- Keru: only the small annular earrings flanking the necklace in the AssamVilla product image. The necklace is omitted and modest wearable fittings are interpreted. The circular opening is preserved.
- Thuriya: the gold-red plug-form pair in the photograph, omitting the adjacent silver-black pair. Floral face, narrow stem and thicker cylindrical back distinguish these from hanging bell earrings.

Patterns, colour combinations, materials and fastening details vary by region, workshop, period and wearer. This pack adds reference-based examples for scene composition; no claim is made that all pieces belong in one costume or have identical forms across centuries.

Exact prompts are in generation-prompts.json. Validation records PNG dimensions, transparency and hashes. The contact-sheet preview is composited on a light background for readability; final individual PNGs preserve alpha transparency.
