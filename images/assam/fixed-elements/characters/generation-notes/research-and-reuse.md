## Purpose

Photorealistic reusable adult performer designs, six views each: front, three-quarter, side, back and two performance poses. These are generated character designs, not documentary photographs or assertions about the identity of the reference models.

Use the reference sheets to preserve face, hair, build, clothing, accessories and instrument scale across later scenes. They establish a reference-based traditional performance costume. Do not automatically insert the exact outfit, decorative motifs, jewellery or stage styling into every historical century.

## Research checks

- [NCERT Kala Utsav textual summary](https://kalautsav.ncert.gov.in/pdf/Kalautsav_Textual_Summary.pdf) identifies Bihu dress and instrument vocabulary, including dhoti, muga mekhela chadar, riha, dhol, pepa, gogona, taal and toka. It does not date the particular styling in the supplied photographs.
- [IGNCA Bihu and Sattriya photographic archive](https://ignca.gov.in/online-digital-resources/digital-images/dance-performances-and-statewise-list-of-tribal-communities/assam-bihu-and-sattriya-gogoi-ranjit-kumar/) identifies a relevant performance-image collection. Used as a research locator; not an attached image input.
- [IIT Guwahati / D'source, Gogona](https://www.dsource.in/resource/popular-folk-musical-instruments/making-0/gogona) documents the bamboo reed instrument and its use as a hair accessory in Bihu. No unsupported attribution of the exact hair ornament in the user photo to a specific subtype.
- [Assam Government Marketing Corporation, product categories](https://agmcltd.assam.gov.in/portlets/handloom-handicraft-products) lists riha separately from mekhela chadar. Do not treat riha and sador as interchangeable names in future asset records.
- [Global InCH, musical instruments of Assam](https://www.globalinch.org/craft/musical-instruments-of-assam/) describes Bihu drum playing with stick and hand, plus associated instruments.

## Reuse controls

- Keep one consistent face and body per character across panels.
- Keep the nasoni's lower mekhela separate from the upper drape; do not turn the outfit into a generic single sari or lehenga.
- Distinguish the decorative fulam jaapi in the nasoni reference from the existing plain farmer's haluwa jaapi.
- Keep the dhulia's dhol across the hips on a shoulder strap, with hide heads at the lateral ends. Stick and open hand play the heads, not the covered barrel shell.
- Period-specific outfits should be produced as separate variants after researching that period.
